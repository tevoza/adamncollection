## Summary

### The Rise of Stoicism - Philosophy Takes an Interest in Life
Consider for a moment, your future self sliding down the deep decline that lives on other side of old age, as you meet your natural end.
Evaluate your existence and consider whether you have lived life well; In a way that allows you to feel at peace.
What does that mean?
The idea: steering your present behavior according to a rule-book, ie a **Philosophy of Life** that allows you to live life well.

The risk is that if we refuse to take on a considered and coherent philosophy of life, then we are likely to *mis-live*.
More precisely, stumble aimlessly reacting and acting in accordance with biological/genetic programming:
- Pursuing and valuing rewards afforded to us via genetic programming; sex, fame, wealth and pleasure - all of which are insatiable and leave no lasting value.
Always wanting a slightly better car, larger house or larger screen etc, finding that what you currently have is never quite good enough.
The idea that truly "making it" or reaching satisfaction just lies in acquiring your next desire. 
Referred to as the _hedonic treadmill_.
The error in this thinking is the reality that we are insatiable.
You'll never reach satisfaction when measuring happiness against such goals.
Religion seems to correlate these pursuits with sin.
- Being consumed by situations we are programmed to be bothered by, allowing primitive emotions such as anger, jealousy, envy etc govern our mentality and response.
For example, the anger experienced when being insulted.
One can let this consume them.
One can also try pause and consider whether you'd like to further indulge in anti-joy, spiting yourself most.

The genetic programming is flawed and can allow you to self-inflict unnecessary suffering.
Why have we been programmed this way?
Probably thanks to evolution.
Those who concern themselves more with the acquiring of resources probably tended to outlive those who didn't.
Hence modern day man therefore inherits these desires.
However, it appears evolution added the continuous desire, without a switch for reaching satisfaction.
As mankind we are fortunately gifted the ability to reason beyond genetic programming towards that which is more meaningful.
But the effort comes in exercising that ability.
That which is genetic and instinctive requires active effort to overcome.

A prevailing philosophy, if you look at the West or Hollywood at least, seems to be "enlightened hedonism": Optimizing for pleasure while making sacrifices to keep it sustainable and socially acceptable.
Very much an embrace of genetic programming with occasional dampeners.
Governed little by what you may truly consider to be meaningful in the long term.
Chasing more expensive taste.
Indulging continuously in cheap dopamine streams.

Stoicism provides a logical framework for understanding and rationalizing through common afflictions of man.
In a way that classic religion has failed to address from my perspective.
And this to me is the most alluring proposition.
It prioritizes **tranquility** over "happiness" as the main goal worth pursuing.
It does however require occasional sacrifice which the enlightened hedonist may be reluctant to forfeit.
